What’s being computed

The section is split into square cells. Walls, the roof and window sashes become solid cells, so the sash angle physically changes the gap air passes through. Each step, air is carried along by its own motion (the semi-Lagrangian method behind Fast Fluid Dynamics), air warmer than outside is pushed upward (Boussinesq buoyancy), floor heat gains warm the air, and a pressure solve keeps the air from piling up or vanishing. Wind enters with a boundary-layer profile, speed at 10 m as set.

Labels at each opening show inflow and outflow through it. A single opening often carries both at once.

What it can’t tell you

A 2D section assumes the building is infinitely long, so air can’t go around the sides; facade pressures are overstated compared with a real building. There’s no turbulence model, and the coarse grid smooths the flow. Walls don’t store or lose heat. Treat patterns and directions as meaningful and speeds as approximate. Air change rates assume the section repeats along a 1 m depth.
